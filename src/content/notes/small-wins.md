I was 26 when I started learning HTML and CSS. I remember feeling embarrassed to share an NFT preview card I’d just finished. It was a basic project. I knew people half my age could probably build it.

I know. I shouldn’t have been comparing myself to others.

Another thing holding me back was the idea that I should work hard in silence and let success make the noise. But Ali Abdaal’s encouragement to share and document the journey helped me think differently.

People can relate to the process, the small wins, and the failures. I didn’t have to wait until I was a senior developer with big software projects before sharing what I was learning. That finished work can be overwhelming when you’re just starting.

Aside from learning from people who’ve already made it, we can learn from people who are just starting, or only a few years ahead. Maybe even run the marathon together.

So I shared the newbie project. A small win. I was turning 27, learning HTML and CSS for the first time, and looking forward to bringing software engineering into my data science projects.

Looking back, I wrote:

> There may be the words “scientist,” “engineer,” and “professor” in my job titles, but one thing I’m actually proud of is never being afraid to be a BEGINNER.

No shame. It’s never too late.
